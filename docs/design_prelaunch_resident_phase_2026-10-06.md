# Design note: a manifest phase that must be fully resident before launch (kernels gang 534f77ba85c4, source-coverage-gap)
Author: pb-integrator, 2026-10-06. Status: CEO APPROVED option A as one bundled change (decision dec-1006-230356-0dca); this note is the design to be reviewed by one Sol pool reviewer BEFORE any code. Evidence packet: /mnt/shared/tessera-measurements/eager-bounded-2200-control-r6-output-only-20261006/{prismabuild-gap.json,terminal-handoff.json}.

## What happened (verified)
Gang 534f77ba85c4 claimed both ranks, then both real preflights refused `ValueError: pinned covers refused: source-coverage-gap`. The data manifest declares 128 files / 175,527,382,717 bytes (163.5 GiB) as ONE phase, `pin_complete_artifact_before_model_start`, because the consumer (LocalArm.pin_inputs) opens all 128 files before either server starts and holds them. The admitted residency map holds 31 entries = 42,556,946,803 bytes (39.6 GiB): the lead chunk only. `covers_for_keys` (reader_lease.py:2036, 2340-2346) correctly refuses: 97 requested keys have no published cover.

## Why (verified in code, not inferred from the map alone)
- `residency_plan.leads_for(plan)` is "its first phase, only": the consumer's admission depends on the FIRST mover of the first phase. The docstring says why: depending on more than the first phase would let the consumer wait on a mover the window has not published yet while the window waits on the consumer's progress to publish it. The window is a rolling one: later chunks and phases are published as the consumer progresses.
- CORRECTION (verified 2026-10-06 from the superseded plan and the queue; the first draft said chunks 1..n were never published, and that was wrong): the plan sealed ONE phase in five stage chunks of 40, 40, 40, 39 and 7 GiB. All five movers were published (each has an intent and an attempts record). Chunk 0 (the lead) ran 125 s and finished at 1791327299; chunk 3 ran 39 s and finished at 1791327341; chunks 1, 2 and 4 were published, never ran, and were withdrawn at the consumer's failure (1791327383). The consumer's rows were published at 166, claimed at 313.96 (14 s after the lead finished) and refused 19 s later at 332. So the window DID publish the rest of the current phase (`window()` publishes the chunks of the phase being read as their turn comes, inside the room); the defect is that the consumer's ADMISSION waits on chunk 0 only, so it claims GPUs while the other 124 GiB is still staging, or is still waiting for room. The coverage never reached a consumer that needed all of it at launch.
- Why the rolling window is the wrong shape for this consumer, in the code: `leads_for` returns only the first mover, deliberately (a consumer waiting on a mover the window publishes only as the consumer progresses is a deadlock). For a phase that is not yet read, `window()` publishes later chunks of the CURRENT phase unconditionally of run-ahead, but still bounded by free room, the tier-advance protection (`permitted`/`gate`), the claim-order walk and the refill horizon. None of that is a promise that the phase lands before the consumer launches.
- Not a capacity problem: the stage tier prismabuild-stage:dl380g10 has a 210 GiB ledger (82 GiB held by others when read, 2026-10-06 ~22:55Z); the artifact is 163.5 GiB, which nearly fills it but fits. The packet's "not proved" list is right that the composed map alone does not show the producer defect; the code reading above does.
- Verified instead of unverified: the tier loop publishes all chunks of the current phase as room allows and keeps them until the consumer's progress passes the phase (a single-phase consumer never passes it, so they are held to its end; unrun ones are withdrawn when it ends). What is NOT verified, and goes to the reviewer: whether publication is reserve-all-or-nothing. `window()` publishes chunk by chunk while `need <= room`, and a published mover takes its tokens only when it is admitted, so two declared consumers on one 210 GiB tier can each hold part of their phase and neither complete (a partial-allocation deadlock). Submission refusal (3) only covers a phase larger than the whole tier.

## Options
A (recommended). An explicit declaration, not an inference: a phase annotation in the data manifest (for example `"resident_before_launch": true`; the manifest already carries per-phase annotations). The plan builder then makes the consumer's leads ALL chunk movers of that phase; the tier loop publishes them all without waiting for consumer progress and keeps their tokens until the consumer ends; and submission REFUSES, before anything is sealed or published, a declared prelaunch phase whose bytes cannot ever fit the stage tier's capacity (clear message; no GPU time wasted). While the chunks wait for tokens the member is refused before its gang code (no election, no fence), as a member with unresolved leads is today. No input dropped, no origin fallback, no budget increase, no seal (a plain declaration in the manifest the consumer already provides). Cost: a new contract across the planner, the tier loop, submission validation, design.md, tests, an independent review, a runtime generation and your yes.
B. Only the fail-fast half of A: refuse at submission a manifest phase that the rolling window cannot make resident at once. Prevents the wasted claim and GPU window, but does not make this run possible. Cheap and safe, and part of A anyway.
C. The consumer opens its files per phase (not all 128 up front), so a rolling window suffices. A change in kernels' LocalArm, outside PrismaBuild; their declared basis is that all 128 are pinned before the servers start.
D. Inference: treat any single phase that spans the whole manifest as prelaunch. Rejected: a single-phase manifest may still stream; an inferred contract is how the earlier exemption lists died.

## Gaps in A to settle in review, not hidden
Whether the tier loop can publish all chunks of one phase concurrently and hold them (needs reading its publication policy: refill horizon, window quarter, prefetch depth); whether holding 163.5 GiB of a 210 GiB tier starves other consumers (it is the point, but it needs a bound and a stated wait); the egress timing (consumer-terminal, as for the last phase); the interaction with gang admission (leads unresolved, no election); and mixed generations (an older tier loop ignores the declaration and publishes as before, so the member would still refuse at preflight rather than corrupt anything).

## Built on earlier history (D33, 2026-10-05/06) and the cost it carries
Gang members with residency leads already have settled semantics: a member whose leads are not resident is refused before the gang code (Q1), its SIBLINGS still elect and fence their hosts while it waits, with an unbounded ready-wait (only the post-claim start barrier is bounded by skew_s; docs/design.md names this as the operational consequence: a window whose movers are slow drains its already-elected siblings' hosts for the whole mover time), and once every lead is executed and pinned and the map is composed the member claims (Q2). Option A relies on exactly that and so inherits the cost, which the first draft did not state: with all chunks of a 163.5 GiB phase as leads, the sibling fence wait grows to the whole staging time, about 6 to 10 minutes at the tier's measured fill of 304 to 509 MB/s (175.5 GB), plus any wait for tokens (82 GiB were held by others when read). It also meets the gang-fence work in flight (D45 gangs at priority 0; PR 1584, with the executive engineer): any bound on that wait belongs there, not here.

## Approved design (CEO, dec-1006-230356-0dca) -- for ONE Sol pool design review before code (D37)
Scope the CEO set: (1) an explicit per-phase prelaunch-resident declaration; (2) leads cover every chunk of a declared phase and the tier loop publishes and holds them until the consumer ends; (3) submission refuses a declared phase larger than the stage tier's total capacity; (4) admission does not claim GPUs until the declared phase is published and resident, so a waiting gang never fences GPUs, and current headroom (82 GiB held by others) is a wait, not a failure; (5) undeclared consumers keep today's streaming behaviour exactly. Keep the #1587 generation decision separate.

Proposed mechanics (each is a question to the reviewer, not a settled fact):
- Declaration lives in the data manifest, so it is part of the manifest's content hash and an operator cannot attach it afterwards. v1: a `resident_before_launch: true` key on an `annotations.phases[i]` entry (those entries are free-form today, so no schema change and older readers ignore it). v2: an optional `resident_before_launch` on a `read_plan.phases[i]` entry, which `core` must add to the phase key set (an older core then refuses the manifest, which fails closed). Only a literal boolean `true` declares; anything else refuses at submission. A reader `storage_tiers.manifest_prelaunch_phases(manifest)` returns the declared names.
- The frozen plan carries the declaration (an optional per-phase key), because tier discovery (`_filed_entry`), admission (`consumer_residency`) and reuse of a frozen plan all derive leads from `leads_for(plan)` with no manifest in hand. A plan with no declaration stays byte-identical to today's. An older plan validator refuses an unknown key and the tier loop files `residency_plan_unreadable` loudly (the #609 precedent), so a mixed-generation fleet fails closed rather than silently streaming.
- `leads_for(plan)`: today's first mover, unless the plan declares phases. Declared phases must be a prefix of the phase order (a prelaunch phase 3 with an unread phase 1 is refused), and the leads are every chunk mover of every declared phase. Everything else stays lazy.
- `window()`: a declared phase is treated as current, outside run-ahead and the refill horizon. Publication of a declared phase is all or nothing against room: nothing publishes until the unpublished bytes of the declared phases fit `free_gib`, and a stall with reason `prelaunch_waiting_for_room` is reported in the tier loop's usual event, so waiting for the 82 GiB is a named wait. This is the partial-allocation guard: without it two declared consumers can each take part of 210 GiB and neither completes. Open question for the reviewer: published movers take tokens at their own admission, not at publication, so is "all fit now" enough, or does a declared consumer need an explicit reservation or an ordering between declared consumers on one tier?
- Hold and release: nothing new, if verified: chunks of a phase the consumer never passes stay until the consumer ends, then the consumer-terminal path (dead-consumer pass, orphan sweep `stage_release`) releases them, and unrun chunks are withdrawn (observed in the incident). The reviewer should check the failed-consumer and withdrawn-consumer paths and a consumer superseded while its leads are mid-copy.
- Submission refusal: in the residency stage sealing path (`pbrun.residency_stage_rows`, also reached by `pbcampaign` and `pbgang`), refuse when the declared phases' total tokens exceed the tier's minted capacity, naming the numbers. Headroom is not checked at submission: a phase that fits the tier but not today's free room waits.
- Gang admission: the claim pass already refuses a member before its election while its leads are unresolved, so with all chunks as leads no member of a gang whose members share movers (the same manifest and tier) elects or fences until the whole phase is resident. Two cases remain: members whose leads differ (a sibling can pass its gates, elect and fence its host while another member's phase is still staging) and the instant between two members' verdicts. Proposal: a pre-election check, only for gangs whose members declare a prelaunch phase, that defers a member's election while any sibling's `residency_verdict` is unresolved. That edit sits in the claim pass beside the gang-fence work in PR 1584 (fix/gang-demand-fence-1579, touching pool.py and docs/design.md), so the reviewer should say whether it goes into this change or follows that PR.
- Undeclared consumers: no code path changes for a plan with no declaration, and a test pins `leads_for`, `window()` and the published row of an undeclared plan to today's output.

Measured stage: the lead chunk took 125 s for 40 GiB (about 340 MB/s); the whole 163.5 GiB phase at the tier's 304-509 MB/s fill is 6 to 10 minutes of waiting before launch, plus any wait for room.

Pending my own check before the PR: confirm in tests that `consumer_residency` and `_filed_entry` return the all-chunk leads for a declared plan, and that reuse of a frozen plan (pbrun 6118) and pbcampaign (1245) publish the same block.

## Revision 1 (after review rev-1006-231016-285d, REQUEST_CHANGES at d131e8ec; Refs #1594)
This section replaces the earlier bullets named in each item. Everything not named stays as above. The reviewer accepted: the incident correction, the contiguous-prefix rule, byte-identical undeclared plans, and strict refusal by an older reader as safe. The reviewer asked: no published-generation or digest identity refusal (that would be a new D32 seal and would couple the separate #1587 decision). Nothing below adds one.

### R1. Reservation: a prelaunch group of advance credits (replaces the `window()` bullet)
Observing room is not reserving it, and the reviewer's counterexample stands: two ~166-token prefixes can both publish against a free 210-token tier. The existing primitive is the advance credit (`window_credit`): tokens held in the tier's own `ResourceLedger` under a deterministic grant holder (`grant_key(consumer, tier, leg, phase, chunk_index)`), bound to a mover by a funding record (`tier-funding/<mover>.<tier>.json`, states reserved -> transferring -> consumed or released), and consumed by the mover's claim so it takes only the remainder from free. That is a durable reservation with no double charge. Today the tier loop fences ONE next leg per window and gates newcomers with `current_min_gib` plus `next_min_gib`. For a declared prefix:
- `advance_needs` reports the whole unpublished declared prefix as the window's current: `current_min_gib` is the sum of every unpublished declared leg, and a new `prelaunch_legs` list names them in read order. `next_min_gib` is the first streaming leg after the prefix, or `None`. The existing newcomer gate (`gate_newcomer`) then counts the whole prefix against held, ready and already-admitted minimums, so a second declared consumer that does not fit beside the first waits (reason `joint-commitment-stall`, transient) and a prefix larger than the tier is `joint-fit-oversize` (permanent), with the same typed reasons the tier loop already files.
- On admission the tier loop reserves one grant per unpublished declared chunk in ONE pass, all or nothing: every `acquire` under the chunk's own grant key, and if any fails, every grant acquired in this pass is released (the newcomer footprint rollback the pass already does). Grants are blind-held until the chunk row publishes, the existing retained-proof rule; the bind is deferred to the pass that sees the row. A pass that dies between acquires leaves a partial group. The next pass retains and tops it up (`reserve_fence` is idempotent: it only ensures the record when the grant already holds enough), and the group is never reclaimed as dangling while its consumer is live. Only the single tier-loop writer per tier does this, in queue scan order, so two consumers cannot interleave partial groups.
- `window()` then publishes every unpublished declared chunk once its group is held. A declared consumer whose group is not held publishes nothing and files `window-stalled` with reason `prelaunch_waiting_for_room` (a wait, not a failure; the 82 GiB held by others is this case). The chunk's claim consumes its own fence through the existing funded-claim path, so nothing is charged twice.
- Declared phases are outside run-ahead and the refill horizon, as in the first draft.

### R2. Retention and release (replaces the 'Hold and release' bullet)
It is not 'nothing new'. Today `window()` evicts passed phases (`residency_plan.py` evict loop) and pressure reclaims beyond-horizon and claim-order ranges (`tier_loop.py` pressure paths). New rules:
- The live declared prefix is excluded from every eviction path: progress-based eviction in `window()`, refill-horizon reclaim and claim-order reclaim in the pressure pass. A multi-phase prefix is held to the consumer's end even after its progress passes the first phase. Streaming phases after the prefix evict as today.
- Byte cleanup is unchanged: consumer terminal, superseded or withdrawn runs the existing paths (dead-consumer pass, orphan sweep `stage_release`, supersession), which stop queued and claimed movers, keep a range another live consumer still reads (shared interest), delay the reap until children finish and defer deletion of a copy still being written.
- Reservation cleanup is separate and new: when the consumer is terminal or superseded, every grant of its group that no mover has consumed is released, and its funding records go to `released`. A grant bound to a mover whose copy is still writing keeps its charge until that copy's stop or terminal proof. A fence a co-owner of a shared mover still depends on is not released by this consumer's end.
- Unrun chunks are withdrawn only when no other live consumer names them. A mid-copy withdrawal follows the in-flight copy rule above.

### R3. Gang joint feasibility (replaces the 'Gang admission' bullet)
- Submission: `pbgang` (it sees every member) sums, per stage tier, the declared prefix tokens of the members' DISTINCT movers (shared ranges deduplicated by `share_namespace(manifest_sha256, tier_id, start, end)`) and refuses when the sum exceeds the tier's minted capacity, naming the tier, the members and the numbers. Headroom is not checked there; below capacity but above free room waits. The per-consumer check in `residency_stage_rows` stays.
- Runtime, same tier: the members of a gang on one tier are one admission unit. The first member's turn gates and reserves the union of their prefixes (deduplicated), all or nothing, as R1; later members' chunks are already covered.
- Runtime, several tiers: each tier has its own single writer, so reservation across tiers is not atomic, and a hold-and-wait cycle between two gangs (G1 holds tier A and waits for B, G2 holds B and waits for A) is possible. Rule: a gang's prelaunch group may be reserved on a tier only when no better-ranked live gang (`_gang.rank`: priority, then earlier publication, then group) still has an unreserved prelaunch group on any tier, so gangs reserve in one global order. A gang that is better-ranked but fully reserved or terminal does not hold others back. This reads only gang records and funding records, which the loop already reads.
- Election: the claim pass defers a member's election while any sibling's `residency_verdict` is unresolved, only for gangs whose members declare a prefix, with denial `deferred_for_gang_prelaunch`. This closes asymmetric leads and stale readiness, so a waiting gang never fences a host (CEO scope (4)). It is part of this change, coordinated with PR 1584, which edits the same claim-pass and gang-fence area: rebase onto it or onto main whichever lands first, and the tests for election order live in this change. Undeclared gangs take no new code path.

### R4. Mixed generations (narrows the earlier compatibility claim; no seal)
- v2 manifests: an older `core` refuses an unknown phase key, so an old submitter cannot produce a plan from a declared v2 manifest. Safe.
- Frozen plans: an older plan validator refuses the new per-phase key, so the tier loop files `residency_plan_unreadable` loudly (the #609 precedent). Safe, not live: the row waits until a supporting loop is deployed. That is an operational requirement (publish before use), not an identity check.
- v1 manifests: the declaration is a free-form annotation, which an old submitter ignores, and it then emits an UNMARKED streaming plan. Refusal of unknown keys is not a safeguard on this path. So the admission side checks the shape instead of the generation: when a worker verifies a consumer's leads, a manifest that declares a prefix while the filed plan does not carry it is refused `residency_prelaunch_undeclared` (denied before any token). The manifest is a CAS blob named by sha and immutable, so the declared prefix is read once and remembered per `(cas root, manifest sha)`. This needs no generation, commit or digest identity.
- Explicit all-chunk leads in the row mean an older admission reader also waits for every chunk instead of launching on chunk 0.

### R5. Prefix and undeclared plans (accepted)
- Declared phases must be a contiguous prefix of the phase order from phase 0. Enforced in manifest parsing (`manifest_prelaunch_phases` refuses a gap or a non-boolean), in frozen-plan validation, and on reuse of a frozen plan (pbrun, pbcampaign).
- `leads_for` expands every declared phase into all its chunk movers.
- A plan with no declaration omits the new key entirely, so its canonical bytes, `plan_sha256` and published rows are byte-identical to today's, as are `window()`, `advance_needs`, gating and eviction. A test pins undeclared plans against recorded outputs.

## Revision 2 (after delta review of Revision 1, REQUEST_CHANGES at b5378a35; Refs #1594)
Findings: (1) group credits need restart accounting and plural protection/cleanup; (2) rank ordering allows a cross-tier hold-and-wait when a higher-priority gang arrives; (3) joint feasibility omits the streaming suffix that must coexist with the retained prefix; (4) the PR body lacked the issue reference. Revision 1's R1 and the gang-runtime bullets of R3 are replaced by the following. All else stands.

### R1'. One atomic group reservation per unit and tier (replaces R1)
The partial-group problem came from reserving one grant per chunk. Remove it: the group is ONE holder per (unit, tier), taken in ONE `ResourceLedger.acquire`, which already takes every token the demand asks for or none. A unit is the consumer, or the gang group when the consumer is a gang member. The holder name is deterministic, `prelaunch-<unit16>-<tier>-<digest>`, never 64-hex and never `claiming.*`, so it cannot collide with an action key or a claimant.
- States per (unit, tier): UNRESERVED (nothing held), RESERVED (the holder holds the whole demand, T tokens), SPLITTING (chunks have been handed to their movers), DONE. There is no partially acquired group, so there is no partial accounting. A crash inside `acquire` is already recovered by the ledger (the private claimant handle is swept; tokens are never free-and-owned at once).
- One new ledger primitive is needed: `ResourceLedger.transfer_count(from_key, to_key, count)`, the per-token rename `transfer` already does but for a named number of tokens, never free in between, idempotent on re-call (it moves what is still short). Today's `transfer` moves a holder's whole reservation, which cannot split a group across its chunk movers. It is tested at ledger level first (interrupted mid-move: the sum is unchanged).
- Per chunk: publish the row; bind the funding record to the group (`reserved`, blind-held, the existing retained-proof rule); `transfer_count` the chunk's tokens to the mover key (`transferring`); the mover's claim consumes them (`consumed`). The mover's claim takes only the remainder from free, which is zero, so nothing is charged twice. Order is the existing one: `transfer_fence` still refuses until the mover has a queue row.
- Demand T is the unit's deduplicated need on that tier: every unpublished declared chunk whose range no live shared mover (#1026) already owns or has staged (`share_namespace(manifest_sha256, tier_id, start, end)`). Ranges already owned are not charged again.
- Accounting (finding 1): a declared consumer whose group holder exists, or any of whose declared chunks are published, is NOT a newcomer (`_is_newcomer` gains that clause, declaration-only). Its group tokens are already in `held_total`, so the gate does not count them again; its planned demand is the streaming suffix only. A newcomer's gate footprint is T plus the suffix minimum of R3'. The restart case (the reviewer's 80 of 166) cannot arise, because the group is all or nothing; the crash that can arise is 'group held, no row published yet', which reads as admitted and publishes next pass.
- Protection (plural, declaration-only branch): for a declared consumer `_protect_tier_advances` takes the group branch and returns positive publication authority for chunk i only when the group holder, or that chunk's already-transferred mover ownership, covers chunk i exactly. The singleton `fence_target` branch is untouched for undeclared consumers and for the suffix of a declared one. Claim-order permission is not a substitute for a held group: a declared consumer without its group publishes nothing and files `window-stalled` with reason `prelaunch_waiting_for_room`.
- Cleanup: the dangling-grant census derives its expected set from the whole live unit before any fit or order early return. For a declared unit the expected set is the group holder per tier plus every published-but-unconsumed chunk's funding record. Unknown evidence retains. When the unit is terminal or superseded the unsplit remainder of the group is released and unconsumed funding goes to `released`; tokens already under a mover follow the mover (a copy still writing keeps its charge until stop or terminal proof; a co-owner's interest in a shared mover keeps it).

### R3'. Gang order: one durable turn for multi-tier units (replaces the R3 runtime bullets)
A unit that needs groups on more than one tier is the only case with a hold-and-wait cycle. A unit on one tier holds nothing partial (R1') and so cannot be part of a cycle; the one tier loop that owns that tier serializes it in queue scan order. For a multi-tier unit:
- Ticket: the first tier loop to see a declared multi-tier unit files `prelaunch-turn/tickets/<unit>.json` (immutable, no-clobber): priority, publication time, and the per-tier demand, deduplicated.
- Turn: `prelaunch-turn/turn-<epoch>.json` names the unit whose turn it is. It is created with a no-clobber link, and that link is the linearization point: two tier loops racing for epoch n resolve to one winner, and every loop reads the same winner. A turn is created only when the previous epoch's turn is `done` (the unit holds every tier group, or is terminal or withdrawn); the creator picks the best-ranked ticket (priority, then ticket time, then unit id) among tickets with no holdings. A higher-priority arrival after a turn exists waits; once a turn names a unit with any holding, nothing overtakes it. That removes the reviewer's counterexample: G2 arriving at higher priority cannot take B while G1 holds the turn.
- Reserving: the turn holder reserves its tier groups in canonical tier-id order, one `acquire` per tier by that tier's own loop, recorded as no-clobber `turn-<epoch>.reserved-<tier>.json`. The last receipt writes `turn-<epoch>.done`. A tier loop reserves for a multi-tier unit only while it is the turn holder; evidence it cannot read (a missing or unreadable ticket, turn or receipt) means no reservation and a named event `prelaunch-turn-unknown`.
- Restart and release: receipts are the durable state, so a restarted loop continues the same epoch. A turn holder that is withdrawn or terminal before `done` releases every group it holds and writes `turn-<epoch>.done` with reason `abandoned`. A turn holder waiting on a tier whose loop is down is reported as `prelaunch-turn-blocked` naming the tier; there is no timeout (#598 took clocks out of this subsystem), withdrawal releases it.
- Cost, stated: reservation of multi-tier prelaunch units is serialized per queue, and a turn holder waiting for room on one tier delays other multi-tier units. A fully reserved group waits on staging and the consumer, never on another prelaunch unit, so no cycle through retained prefixes exists. No starvation-freedom claim, as elsewhere in this subsystem.

### R3''. Joint feasibility includes the streaming suffix (finding 3)
Retained prefix plus suffix progress is what must fit, not the prefix alone. Eviction is by passed phase and the prefix is never evicted, so define for a plan with prefix tokens T and suffix phases p1..pn (stage_gib each): `B = T + max over i of (size(pi) + size(p(i+1)))`, with `size(p(n+1)) = 0` and `B = T` when there is no suffix. This is the conservative two-phase overlap, the same `step` the run-ahead bound already uses.
- Submission (`residency_stage_rows` and `pbgang`): refuse when B exceeds the tier's minted capacity, naming T, the suffix term, and the capacity. For a gang, sum B per tier over the members' DISTINCT movers (shared ranges counted once, by namespace). The regression case: prefix fits, first suffix leg fits, later progress is impossible (160 + 40 + 40 > 210) refuses at submission.
- Runtime gate: a newcomer's footprint is T plus the first two suffix legs (the existing `current_min_gib` plus `next_min_gib` of the suffix), against held, ready and admitted windows' protected nexts, which is what the gate already counts. After admission the prefix is held tokens in the ledger and the suffix rolls under the existing singleton protection. Unrelated current occupancy is a wait, an impossible bound is a refusal.
