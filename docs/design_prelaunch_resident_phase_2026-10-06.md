# Design note: a manifest phase that must be fully resident before launch (kernels gang 534f77ba85c4, source-coverage-gap)
Author: pb-integrator, 2026-10-06. Status: CEO APPROVED option A as one bundled change (decision dec-1006-230356-0dca); this note is the design to be reviewed by one Sol pool reviewer BEFORE any code. Evidence packet: /mnt/shared/tessera-measurements/eager-bounded-2200-control-r6-output-only-20261006/{prismabuild-gap.json,terminal-handoff.json}.

## What happened (verified)
Gang 534f77ba85c4 claimed both ranks, then both real preflights refused `ValueError: pinned covers refused: source-coverage-gap`. The data manifest declares 128 files / 175,527,382,717 bytes (163.5 GiB) as ONE phase, `pin_complete_artifact_before_model_start`, because the consumer (LocalArm.pin_inputs) opens all 128 files before either server starts and holds them. The admitted residency map holds 31 entries = 42,556,946,803 bytes (39.6 GiB): the lead chunk only. `covers_for_keys` (reader_lease.py:2036, 2340-2346) correctly refuses: 97 requested keys have no published cover.

## Why (verified in code, not inferred from the map alone)
- `residency_plan.leads_for(plan)` is "its first phase, only": the consumer's admission depends on the FIRST mover of the first phase. The docstring says why: depending on more than the first phase would let the consumer wait on a mover the window has not published yet while the window waits on the consumer's progress to publish it. The window is a rolling one: later chunks and phases are published as the consumer progresses.
- CORRECTION (verified 2026-10-06 from the superseded plan and the queue; the first draft said chunks 1..n were never published, and that was wrong): the plan sealed ONE phase in five stage chunks of 40, 40, 40, 39 and 7 GiB. All five movers were published (each has an intent and an attempts record). Chunk 0 (the lead) ran 125 s and finished at 1791327299; chunk 3 ran 39 s and finished at 1791327341; chunks 1, 2 and 4 were published, never ran, and were withdrawn at the consumer's failure (1791327383). The consumer's rows were published at 166, claimed at 313.96 (14 s after the lead finished) and refused 19 s later at 332. So the window DID publish the rest of the current phase (`window()` publishes the chunks of the phase being read as their turn comes, inside the room); the defect is that the consumer's ADMISSION waits on chunk 0 only, so it claims GPUs while the other 124 GiB is still staging, or is still waiting for room. The coverage never reached a consumer that needed all of it at launch.
- Why the rolling window is the wrong shape for this consumer, in the code: `leads_for` returns only the first mover, deliberately (a consumer waiting on a mover the window publishes only as the consumer progresses is a deadlock). For a phase that is not yet read, `window()` publishes later chunks of the CURRENT phase unconditionally of run-ahead, but still bounded by free room, the tier-advance protection (`permitted`/`gate`), the claim-order walk and the refill horizon. None of that is a promise that the phase lands before the consumer launches.
- Not a capacity problem: the stage tier prismabuild-stage:dl380g10 has a 210 GiB ledger (82 GiB held by others when read, 2026-10-06 ~22:55Z); the artifact is 163.5 GiB, which nearly fills it but fits. The packet's "not proved" list is right that the composed map alone does not show the producer defect; the code reading above does.
- Verified instead of unverified: the tier loop publishes all chunks of the current phase as room allows and keeps them until the consumer's progress passes the phase (a single-phase consumer never passes it, so they are held to its end; unrun ones are withdrawn when it ends). What is NOT verified, and goes to the reviewer: whether publication is reserve-all-or-nothing. `window()` publishes chunk by chunk while `need <= room`, and a published mover takes its tokens only when it is admitted, so two declared consumers on one 210 GiB tier can each hold part of their phase and neither complete (a partial-allocation deadlock). Submission refusal (3) only covers a phase larger than the whole tier.

## Options
A (recommended). An explicit declaration, not an inference: a phase annotation in the data manifest (for example `"resident_before_launch": true`; the manifest already carries per-phase annotations). The plan builder then makes the consumer's leads ALL chunk movers of that phase; the tier loop publishes them all without waiting for consumer progress and keeps their tokens until the consumer ends; and submission REFUSES, before anything is sealed or published, a declared prelaunch phase whose bytes cannot ever fit the stage tier's capacity (clear message; no GPU time wasted). While the chunks wait for tokens the member is refused before its gang code (no election, no fence), as a member with unresolved leads is today. No input dropped, no origin fallback, no budget increase, no seal (a plain declaration in the manifest the consumer already provides). Cost: a new contract across the planner, the tier loop, submission validation, design.md, tests, an independent review, a runtime generation and your yes.
B. Only the fail-fast half of A: refuse at submission a manifest phase that the rolling window cannot make resident at once. Prevents the wasted claim and GPU window, but does not make this run possible. Cheap and safe, and part of A anyway.
C. The consumer opens its files per phase (not all 128 up front), so a rolling window suffices. A change in kernels' LocalArm, outside PrismaBuild; their declared basis is that all 128 are pinned before the servers start.
D. Inference: treat any single phase that spans the whole manifest as prelaunch. Rejected: a single-phase manifest may still stream; an inferred contract is how the earlier exemption lists died.

## Gaps in A to settle in review, not hidden
Whether the tier loop can publish all chunks of one phase concurrently and hold them (needs reading its publication policy: refill horizon, window quarter, prefetch depth); whether holding 163.5 GiB of a 210 GiB tier starves other consumers (it is the point, but it needs a bound and a stated wait); the egress timing (consumer-terminal, as for the last phase); the interaction with gang admission (leads unresolved, no election); and mixed generations (an older tier loop ignores the declaration and publishes as before, so the member would still refuse at preflight rather than corrupt anything).

## Built on earlier history (D33, 2026-10-05/06) and the cost it carries
Gang members with residency leads already have settled semantics: a member whose leads are not resident is refused before the gang code (Q1), its SIBLINGS still elect and fence their hosts while it waits, with an unbounded ready-wait (only the post-claim start barrier is bounded by skew_s; docs/design.md names this as the operational consequence: a window whose movers are slow drains its already-elected siblings' hosts for the whole mover time), and once every lead is executed and pinned and the map is composed the member claims (Q2). Option A relies on exactly that and so inherits the cost, which the first draft did not state: with all chunks of a 163.5 GiB phase as leads, the sibling fence wait grows to the whole staging time, about 6 to 10 minutes at the tier's measured fill of 304 to 509 MB/s (175.5 GB), plus any wait for tokens (82 GiB were held by others when read). It also meets the gang-fence work in flight (D45 gangs at priority 0; PR 1584, with the executive engineer): any bound on that wait belongs there, not here.

## Approved design (CEO, dec-1006-230356-0dca) -- for ONE Sol pool design review before code (D37)
Scope the CEO set: (1) an explicit per-phase prelaunch-resident declaration; (2) leads cover every chunk of a declared phase and the tier loop publishes and holds them until the consumer ends; (3) submission refuses a declared phase larger than the stage tier's total capacity; (4) admission does not claim GPUs until the declared phase is published and resident, so a waiting gang never fences GPUs, and current headroom (82 GiB held by others) is a wait, not a failure; (5) undeclared consumers keep today's streaming behaviour exactly. Keep the #1587 generation decision separate.

Proposed mechanics (each is a question to the reviewer, not a settled fact):
- Declaration lives in the data manifest, so it is part of the manifest's content hash and an operator cannot attach it afterwards. v1: a `resident_before_launch: true` key on an `annotations.phases[i]` entry (those entries are free-form today, so no schema change and older readers ignore it). v2: an optional `resident_before_launch` on a `read_plan.phases[i]` entry, which `core` must add to the phase key set (an older core then refuses the manifest, which fails closed). Only a literal boolean `true` declares; anything else refuses at submission. A reader `storage_tiers.manifest_prelaunch_phases(manifest)` returns the declared names.
- The frozen plan carries the declaration (an optional per-phase key), because tier discovery (`_filed_entry`), admission (`consumer_residency`) and reuse of a frozen plan all derive leads from `leads_for(plan)` with no manifest in hand. A plan with no declaration stays byte-identical to today's. An older plan validator refuses an unknown key and the tier loop files `residency_plan_unreadable` loudly (the #609 precedent), so a mixed-generation fleet fails closed rather than silently streaming.
- `leads_for(plan)`: today's first mover, unless the plan declares phases. Declared phases must be a prefix of the phase order (a prelaunch phase 3 with an unread phase 1 is refused), and the leads are every chunk mover of every declared phase. Everything else stays lazy.
- `window()`: a declared phase is treated as current, outside run-ahead and the refill horizon. Publication of a declared phase is all or nothing against room: nothing publishes until the unpublished bytes of the declared phases fit `free_gib`, and a stall with reason `prelaunch_waiting_for_room` is reported in the tier loop's usual event, so waiting for the 82 GiB is a named wait. This is the partial-allocation guard: without it two declared consumers can each take part of 210 GiB and neither completes. Open question for the reviewer: published movers take tokens at their own admission, not at publication, so is "all fit now" enough, or does a declared consumer need an explicit reservation or an ordering between declared consumers on one tier?
- Hold and release: nothing new, if verified: chunks of a phase the consumer never passes stay until the consumer ends, then the consumer-terminal path (dead-consumer pass, orphan sweep `stage_release`) releases them, and unrun chunks are withdrawn (observed in the incident). The reviewer should check the failed-consumer and withdrawn-consumer paths and a consumer superseded while its leads are mid-copy.
- Submission refusal: in the residency stage sealing path (`pbrun.residency_stage_rows`, also reached by `pbcampaign` and `pbgang`), refuse when the declared phases' total tokens exceed the tier's minted capacity, naming the numbers. Headroom is not checked at submission: a phase that fits the tier but not today's free room waits.
- Gang admission: the claim pass already refuses a member before its election while its leads are unresolved, so with all chunks as leads no member of a gang whose members share movers (the same manifest and tier) elects or fences until the whole phase is resident. Two cases remain: members whose leads differ (a sibling can pass its gates, elect and fence its host while another member's phase is still staging) and the instant between two members' verdicts. Proposal: a pre-election check, only for gangs whose members declare a prelaunch phase, that defers a member's election while any sibling's `residency_verdict` is unresolved. That edit sits in the claim pass beside the gang-fence work in PR 1584 (fix/gang-demand-fence-1579, touching pool.py and docs/design.md), so the reviewer should say whether it goes into this change or follows that PR.
- Undeclared consumers: no code path changes for a plan with no declaration, and a test pins `leads_for`, `window()` and the published row of an undeclared plan to today's output.

Measured stage: the lead chunk took 125 s for 40 GiB (about 340 MB/s); the whole 163.5 GiB phase at the tier's 304-509 MB/s fill is 6 to 10 minutes of waiting before launch, plus any wait for room.
